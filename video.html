Part 2: Instructional Screencast

One Box, Three Ways of Thinking

A short walkthrough that isolates Describe, Analyse and Evaluate on a single concrete object.

Video Walkthrough: Deconstructing Command Terms

Watch this 1:46 screencast before the collaborative workshop.

  • 0:00–0:11 Welcome back! Today, we’re solving a huge exam problem: how do you know if a prompt wants you to describe, analyze, or evaluate? Let’s use this single box to figure it out.
  • 0:11–0:36 Level 1: Describe — Look at the outside of the box and state observable facts. It’s a brown wooden box, 30 cm wide, with a silver latch. You give zero opinion. In Legal Studies, describing means stating the facts, features, or rules of a legal topic.
  • 0:36–1:16 Level 2: Analyze — Describing only shows us the outside, but analyzing requires us to open the box up. Look inside and examine how the internal parts work together. The latch connects to the lock mechanism to keep the contents secure. In Legal Studies, analyzing means breaking down how legal components work together—like how judge directions keep a jury fair. You must use cause-and-effect words like “this leads to” or “as a result” to show connections. Don’t just list legal rules; explain how one feature directly impacts another.
  • 1:16–1:35 Level 3: Evaluate — Put the box on a scale, weigh up the pros and cons. The lock is sturdy (a strength), but the hinge jams easily (a weakness). Overall, it works well. Evaluating requires a final judgment. You must weigh up arguments for and against before deciding overall effectiveness.
  • 1:35–1:46 Summary — Same box, three completely different ways of thinking. Ready to practice this on actual exam prompts? Scroll down to Section 3 on our website to start writing.
